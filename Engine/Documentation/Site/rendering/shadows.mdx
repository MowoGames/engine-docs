Mowo layers several shadow techniques. Every tier has cascaded shadow maps for the sun and an atlas for local lights. Higher tiers add soft penumbrae, virtual shadow maps, stochastic shadows for many lights and ray-traced sun shadows. Each layer falls back to the one below it where it cannot answer.

## Which technique runs where

| Technique | Lights | Low | Medium | High | Epic | Cinematic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cascaded shadow maps | Sun | 2 cascades, 1024 | 3 cascades, 2048 | 4 cascades, 2048 | 4 cascades, 2048 | 4 cascades, 2048 |
| Local shadows | Spot, point, rect | Atlas, 16 views | Atlas, 32 views | Atlas, 48 views | Virtual pages, 128 views | Virtual pages, 128 views |
| Soft shadows (PCSS) | All shadowed lights | — | — | on | on | on |
| SDF far sun shadows | Sun, past the cascade range | — | on | on | on | on |
| Cheap fallback for over-budget lights | Local lights | on | on | on | — | — |
| Virtual shadow maps | Sun | — | — | — | on | on |
| MegaLights | Local lights past the budget | — | — | — | on | on |
| Ray-traced sun shadows | Sun | — | — | — | — | on |
| Cloud shadows | Sun, raymarched clouds | — | — | on | on | on |

Tier values come from. Contact and capsule shadows are covered on the [Lights](/docs/rendering/lights/#contact-shadows) page.

The main settings are on **Project Settings > Engine > Shadows**, and the player's settings screen has **Shadow Map Method**, **Shadow Map Size**, **Shadow Distance**, **MegaLights** and **Ray-Traced Sun Shadows** rows.

## Sun: cascaded shadow maps

The sun (more exactly, the brightest directional light above the horizon; see [Lights](/docs/rendering/lights/#several-directional-lights)) renders into a 2×2 atlas of cascades, one tile per cascade. At Epic and Cinematic the virtual shadow map serves the meshes and the cascades are its fallback and the terrain and foliage authority, so their tiles are 2048 like High's (an 8192 square atlas of 256 MiB became 4096 square and 64 MiB; raise `r.Shadow.Resolution` if terrain shadows need more). The cascade range is `r.Shadow.MaxDistance` (default 150 world units); a scene's World Settings shadow distance overrides it when set. The shadow fades out over the last 15% of the range instead of cutting off.

Each cascade is fitted to a bounding sphere and snapped to whole texels, so shadow edges do not crawl as the camera moves (`r.Shadow.StableFit`, default 1). Static casters are cached per cascade and redrawn only when the cascade moves or a static caster in it changes (`r.Shadow.CsmStaticCache`, default 1); each frame then draws only skinned and moving casters on top.

| cvar | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `r.Shadow.Resolution` | tier | Per-cascade tile size, 256 to 8192. The atlas is recreated live. |
| `r.Shadow.Cascades` | tier | Cascades rendered, 1 to 4 |
| `r.Shadow.MaxDistance` | 150 | Sun shadow range in world units |
| `r.Shadow.CsmStaticCache` | 1 | Cache static casters per cascade |
| `r.Shadow.CascadeDither` | 1 | With a temporal resolve (TAA or an upscaler), the 15% transition band picks one of the two cascades per pixel by noise instead of evaluating both and blending: half the filter taps for the same averaged result. 0 always blends |

The soft penumbra is clamped to the same width in world space in every cascade, so it no longer steps at a cascade seam.

## Local lights

Spot, point and rect lights that cast shadows compete for a shared budget each frame. Every light is scored by its projected size and brightness; the score decides who wins when the budget is short. A point light uses six views, one per cube face, and so does a Rect light: it lights a full hemisphere (both sides when two-sided), so one frustum along its axis would leave everything past a wide angle unshadowed. Every extended material lobe (clear coat, anisotropy, substrate, hair, subsurface, cloth, foliage transmission, eye) takes the same shadow, IES and cookie as the base lobe; on Direct3D 11 those lobes are still unshadowed from local lights, because the shader compiler there cannot build the shadowed path in a reasonable time. The tier caps how many views a frame may render (`r.Shadow.MaxLocalViews`). A light that misses the budget is handed to MegaLights where it is on, gets a cheap screen-space shadow below Epic (`r.MegaLights.CheapMode`), or otherwise shades unshadowed.

Hand-overs between lights fade over a few frames, and a light that lost its slot waits before competing again, so lights at the budget boundary do not flicker.

### Up to High: the atlas

Below Epic, local shadows render into tiles of one atlas, between `r.Shadow.LocalMinTile` and `r.Shadow.LocalTileSize` pixels. A tile's size follows its light's size on screen: `r.Shadow.LocalTexelsPerPixel` (default 1) shadow texels per pixel of the lit sphere, rounded to a power of two inside the tier's limits. Only when the atlas overflows do the least important tiles halve first. Set it to 0 for the older sizing relative to the top-ranked light.

A light keeps its tiles from frame to frame: another light entering, leaving or changing size no longer moves them, and the allocator repacks only when a tile cannot be placed around the ones that stay. Tiles whose light and casters did not change are not redrawn (`r.Shadow.LocalCache`, default 1), so a static scene redraws no tiles after the first frame. The cache is keyed by the light (its entity, so adding or removing another light never renumbers it) and cube face, and a skinned character only dirties the tiles its posed bounds reach, so an animated character on the other side of the level costs the atlas nothing.

When a skinned or dynamic-body caster is in the scene, the atlas also gets a second layer (`r.Shadow.LocalAtlas.TwoLayer`, default 1): a static-depth atlas of the same size, allocated only then. A tile under a moving character restores its kept static depth and redraws just the moving casters instead of every caster in the tile. The layer stays for `r.Shadow.LocalAtlas.TwoLayerHoldFrames` (180) frames after the last dynamic caster leaves, so a character stepping in and out does not rebuild the atlas each time.

### Bias

Local lights bias like the sun. The receiver is pushed off its surface along the normal by the shadow texel size at its depth, more on grazing surfaces, and every filter tap is biased by the receiver plane's rise over its offset, in linear depth. **Shadow Bias Scale** scales that model (1 is the default); it is no longer an offset in projected depth, so values tuned for the old behaviour may want a second look.

### Epic and up: virtual pages

At Epic and Cinematic each local shadow view is a sparse virtual map of 128-texel pages (`r.Shadow.Local.Virtual`). Only pages under a receiver the camera can see are resident, and they stay resident until their light or casters change. A second layer keeps each page's static depth, so a page under a moving character redraws only the moving caster (`r.Shadow.Local.Virtual.TwoLayer`). `r.Shadow.Local.VirtualMaxPages` (default 16, 2048 texels) bounds the largest map. Set `r.Shadow.Local.Virtual 0` to use the atlas at any tier.

## Per-light shadow settings

These fields are in the **Shadows** category of a light:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cast Shadows | Whether the light casts shadows. On by default. |
| Shadow Bias Scale | Multiplier on the light's shadow bias. Above 1 removes acne; below 1 tightens contact at some acne risk. The virtual shadow map ignores it, because its bias is computed exactly. |
| Shadow Resolution Bias | Moves a local light's shadow size up or down by whole power-of-two steps (−2 to +2), within the tier's limits. |
| Shadow Near Plane | Near clip of a local light's shadow projection in metres (default 0.1). Raise it when a large light clips nearby geometry into its own shadow. |

## Soft shadows (PCSS)

From High up, shadows use percentage-closer soft shadows: sharp where the occluder touches the receiver and wide where it is far. The penumbra width comes from the light's size:

- Directional light: **Source Angle** (degrees).
- Spot and point lights: **Source Radius** (metres). Area lights use their width and height.

A size of 0, or `r.Shadow.Soft 0`, restores the fixed filter kernel. The penumbra is capped in shadow-map texels by `r.Shadow.MaxPenumbraTexels`; `r.Shadow.BlockerSamples` and `r.Shadow.FilterSamples` set the tap counts.

### Give the sun a soft shadow

<Steps>
<Step title="Use High or above">
PCSS is a High and up feature. Force it on a lower tier with `r.Shadow.Soft 1`.
</Step>
<Step title="Set the Source Angle">
Select the directional light, open **Shape** in the Details panel and raise **Source Angle**. Larger values give wider penumbrae farther from the contact point.
</Step>
<Step title="Check the ceiling">
If very distant occluders still look sharp, the penumbra is hitting `r.Shadow.MaxPenumbraTexels`. Raise it with care: it widens the filter.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Virtual shadow maps for the sun

At Epic and up the sun also renders into a virtual shadow map: a clipmap of camera-anchored levels over a shared page pool. Only pages under visible receivers get memory, static casters render once into cached pages, and moving or skinned casters re-render their pages. The resolve traces several short rays across the sun's disc through the map for soft, contact-hardening shadows (`r.VSM.SmrtRays`).

Where virtual pages cover a pixel, they decide the sun shadow. Where they do not (an unmapped or stale page, past the coarsest level), the cascades answer. At most `r.VSM.PageBudget` pages are redrawn per frame, so a sun move never hitches; stale pages show the cascade shadow until they are redrawn.

<Callout type="note">
GPU foliage and terrain are not drawn into virtual pages, so their shadows come from the cascades. Once the virtual map is steady, the cascades draw only terrain and GPU foliage (`r.VSM.CsmCasters`).
</Callout>

`r.VSM` forces the layer on (`1`) or off (`0`) at any tier; `-1` follows the tier.

Static meshes with an import-built cluster hierarchy can render into the virtual map through the mesh-shading caster (`r.Meshlet.VsmCasters`, off by default). Each clipmap level cuts the hierarchy at its own texel density and culls clusters against the page it draws, so a distant level carries coarser casters than the near one. Masked, double-sided, skinned and instanced casters keep the classic draw. The switch is off until frame-time measurements show it paying for itself; the shadow it casts matches the classic caster's.

The clipmap adds `r.VSM.Clipmap.ExtraLevels` (default 1) levels past the one that covers `r.Shadow.MaxDistance`, so meshes keep casting shadows beyond the cascades. The coarsest `r.VSM.CoarseLevels` levels (default 2) keep a block of `r.VSM.CoarseBlockPages` pages (default 12) around the camera mapped even where no opaque pixel needs them, so volumetric fog and translucent surfaces in the open air get the virtual map's shadows instead of the mesh-less cascades. Beyond that block the cascades still answer.

## SDF far shadows

Past the cascade range, the sun does not fall back to fully lit from Medium up. It cone-traces the distance field toward the sun, using the light's Source Angle as the cone (`r.Shadow.SdfFar`, `r.Shadow.SdfFarSteps`). The distance field is centred on the camera and covers a limited range, so this helps most in dense interiors and scenes with a short shadow distance.

## Ray-traced sun shadows

At Cinematic, a pre-pass casts `r.RTShadows.Samples` rays per pixel inside the sun's disc through the ray-tracing scene and accumulates them over frames. The lighting reads the result instead of the cascade and virtual-map lookup, so contact shadows are exact with no cascade seams and no bias to tune. Past the cascade fade the result is combined with the SDF far shadow.

- It needs ray query. On D3D12 and Vulkan it uses a ray-tracing pipeline whose hit groups alpha-test masked casters exactly; on Metal it uses an inline ray query.
- It runs on the primary view only; secondary editor views keep the cascades.
- Force it on another tier with `r.RTShadows 1`.

See [Ray tracing](/docs/rendering/ray-tracing/).

## Many shadowed lights: MegaLights

The budget bounds how many local lights get a shadow map. MegaLights, at Epic and Cinematic in a project that turns on **MegaLights** (Project Settings > Rendering > Features, off for a new project), takes the shadow-casting lights that lost the budget and shades them stochastically: each pixel importance-samples a few of them (`r.MegaLights.Samples`), traces one shadow ray per sample, then denoises over time and space (`r.MegaLights.Denoise`). The deferred lighting pass skips those lights, so nothing counts twice. Shadow rays use the ray-tracing scene when one exists and a screen-space depth march otherwise.

<Callout type="warning">
MegaLights is a sampling and denoising implementation, not a full ReSTIR stack. Expect visible noise at one sample per pixel, and note that lights shaded by MegaLights ignore their light function.
</Callout>

### Light a scene with hundreds of lamps

<Steps>
<Step title="Use Epic or force MegaLights">
Turn on **MegaLights** in Project Settings > Rendering > Features, then switch to Epic or set `r.MegaLights 1`.
</Step>
<Step title="Mark the lights as shadow casters">
Turn on **Cast Shadows** on each light. The most important lights still get shadow maps; the rest are shaded by MegaLights.
</Step>
<Step title="Add a ray scene for better occlusion">
With any ray-traced feature active, MegaLights traces against the ray-tracing scene instead of the depth buffer.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Cloud shadows

Raymarched clouds (Epic and up; lower tiers draw a 2.5D cloud plane) build a sun-space transmittance map of the cloud layer and multiply it into the sun term and the volumetric fog (`r.Clouds.Shadows`). `r.Clouds.ShadowStrength` blends it and `r.Clouds.ShadowFootprint` sets the area it covers around the camera.

## Casters and receivers

The mesh's **Cast Shadows** toggle decides whether it casts in every shadow system. Alpha-masked cutouts cast through their mask. Alpha-blended meshes with Cast Shadows on cast a partial, dithered shadow in proportion to their opacity (`r.Shadow.Translucent`, default 1). A mesh with **Receive Shadows** off skips every shadow term on its pixels.

Translucent surfaces and water receive the same shadows as opaque ones: the sun's cascades with soft shadows, the virtual shadow map where it covers them, SDF far shadows, cloud shadows, and the local shadow of every point, spot and Rect light. MegaLights shades opaque pixels only, so a light it manages shadows a translucent surface with a short screen-space ray (up to 4 m) instead.

### Skinned casters and the skin cache

By default a skinned caster blends the bone palette in the vertex shader of every pass that draws it: once per cascade, once per local-light cube face. `r.SkinCache 1` skins each animated mesh once into a per-pose vertex buffer with a compute pre-skin instead. The cascades and the local-light atlas then draw that buffer through the same pipelines static meshes use, and the skinned meshlet path reads the same buffer, so one creature costs one skinning pass however many shadow views it appears in. A pose that did not change is not skinned again. The cache is off by default (the previous behaviour) until frame-time measurements say it pays for itself on your target.

Casters whose material has its own shadow graph (an alpha-masked or displaced graph), the virtual shadow map, velocity, selection and decal passes keep skinning for themselves.

## Contact shadows

The sun's contact shadow (a light's **Contact Shadow Length**, see [Lights](/docs/rendering/lights/#contact-shadows)) is marched once per pixel in a compute pass into a mask the lighting reads (`r.ContactShadows.Compute`, default 1), and pixels facing away from the sun skip the march. Other lights, and the sun when the pass is off or on a secondary view, march inline in the lighting shader. Both use the same soft march.

## Debug views

The viewport's **View Mode** menu (and the Render Debug panel) has three shadow views next to Shadow Cascades:

| View | Shows |
|---|---|
| Virtual Shadow Map | The sun's clipmap per pixel: one hue per level, dark gray for an unmapped page, red for a stale page the cascades are serving, purple for an excluded-caster page, a dimmed hue when the coarse parent level answered. Magenta when the virtual map is off |
| Local Shadow Tiles | The nearest shadowed local light's owner hue, dimmed by its residency fade and desaturated for small tiles. Red marks a light that lost its tile |
| Shadow Light Complexity | How many shadow-casting lights reach the pixel's cluster, from black (none) through green, yellow and red to white (8 or more) |

The viewport **Stats** overlay also lists the shadow draw calls, the sun's virtual pages (mapped and redrawn), and the local shadows (lights, tiles or pages, how many redrew, and how many lights have no tile).

## Limits

- Translucent surfaces get no contact or capsule shadows, and the screen-space ray that stands in for MegaLights only sees occluders that are on screen.
- The virtual shadow maps do not draw GPU foliage, terrain or materials with world-position offset; those shadows come from the cascades.
- SDF far shadows only reach as far as the distance field.
- Area-light shadows are a projection from the light's centre, not a true area solve.
