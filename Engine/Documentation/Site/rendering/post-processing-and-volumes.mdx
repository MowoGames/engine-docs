The post-processing chain turns the lit HDR image into the final frame: depth of field, motion blur, auto-exposure, bloom, tonemapping, color grading, lens flare and sharpening. Each scene has a baseline set of post-process values. **Volumes** override those values in regions of the world, or everywhere, and blend between each other as the camera moves.

## The post chain

<Flow steps={["DoF", "Motion blur", "Auto-exposure", "Bloom", "Temporal upscale", "Tonemap + grading", "Post-process material", "TAA", "Lens flare", "FXAA / CAS"]} highlight={5} caption="Post-processing order. See Frame pipeline for where it sits in the frame." />

| Effect | Notes | Main controls |
|---|---|---|
| Depth of field | Circle of confusion from depth; a separable gaussian below Epic, a circular bokeh disk at Epic and Cinematic | `r.DoF.Bokeh` (-1 tier, 0 gaussian, 1 disk) |
| Motion blur | Tile-based; uses per-object velocity when available. Off at Low. Skipped on a view's first frame, on a camera cut (a jump of more than 25 m or a turn past 90 degrees) and on the frame after a stall of more than 0.25 s, so a teleport or a load hitch never smears the picture. | `r.MotionBlurObject` (default 1) |
| Auto-exposure | Runs before bloom, so the bloom threshold works on exposed luminance | `r.AutoExposure.*` |
| Local exposure | Lifts shadows and holds highlights locally after lighting | `r.LocalExposure` (-1 scene, 0 off, 1 on) |
| Bloom | A mip chain of 4 to 6 levels depending on tier. Off at Low. | `r.Bloom.Mips` |
| Tonemap | See below | `r.Tonemap` |
| Color grading | Contrast, saturation, white balance, lift-gamma-gain, a 3D LUT, chromatic aberration, film grain | `r.ColorGradingLUT` (a `.cube` path) |
| Vignette | Darkens the frame edges before the tonemap, like a lens. Intensity scales the darkening and is bounded, so the corners never fall below half brightness; the default is subtle. | Volume and scene settings |
| Post-process material | A PostProcess-domain material evaluated as a fullscreen pass after tonemapping | `r.PostProcessMaterial` |
| Lens flare | Screen-space ghosts and halo | Volume and scene settings |
| Sharpening | Contrast-adaptive sharpening in the final pass | `r.Sharpness` (percent) |

The project-wide defaults are on **Project Settings > Engine > Post Process**.

### Tonemapping

`r.Tonemap` overrides the scene's operator:

| Value | Operator |
|---|---|
| -1 | The scene's setting |
| 0 | ACES (fitted curve) |
| 1 | AgX |
| 2 | Reinhard |
| 3 | Clamp |
| 4 | ACES reference transform with an output transform for the display () |
| 5 | A display LUT from `r.DisplayLUT` or the scene, a `.cube` file indexed in log2 stops around mid-grey; falls back to 4 without one |

For HDR displays, see [Display, frame rate and HDR](/docs/rendering/display-and-hdr/#hdr-output).

### Exposure

Manual exposure is a multiplier, default 1.8. Turn on the **physical camera** in the scene's post-process settings to expose from f-stop, shutter time and ISO instead; the multiplier is then ignored.

Auto-exposure is on at every tier. Metering is average, spot, center-weighted or a histogram percentile window (`r.AutoExposure.Metering`). The variables below override the scene's values for testing:

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `r.AutoExposure.EV` | Compensation in stops added on top of the scene's |
| `r.AutoExposure.MinEV`, `r.AutoExposure.MaxEV` | Limits of the adapted exposure in stops. Lower the maximum to stop a dark scene being lifted to grey. |
| `r.AutoExposure.SpeedUp`, `r.AutoExposure.SpeedDown` | Adaptation rates toward brighter and darker scenes |
| `r.AutoExposure.LowPercent`, `r.AutoExposure.HighPercent` | The percentile window for histogram metering |

The exposure limits belong on volumes in a real scene: a dark room can lower the ceiling and release it outdoors.

### Accessibility

After the tonemap, `r.ColorBlind.Mode` applies color-vision correction (1 protanopia, 2 deuteranopia, 3 tritanopia) at `r.ColorBlind.Strength`. `r.ColorBlind.Simulate` previews the frame as a viewer with that deficiency sees it. `r.DisplayGamma` (1.8 to 2.6, default 2.2) re-encodes the SDR image for the player's panel.

## Volumes

A volume is a zone that overrides post-process settings. Each setting in a volume profile is a slot with an **override** flag, and only the flagged slots change anything (<Source symbol="VolumeProfile" />).

### Place a volume

In the editor's **Place Actors** palette, the **Volumes** category has **Post Process Volume** (`APostProcessVolume`) and the generic **Volume** (`AVolumeActor`). They are the same actor: a post-process volume is a volume under the name Unreal users look for.

### The volume component

| Field | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Global | on/off | A global volume always contributes. A local volume contributes when the camera is inside its box or within its Blend Distance. On by default. |
| Half Extents | 0.1 to 1000 | Half-size of the box around the entity's position. Used only when not global. |
| Priority | -100 to 100 | Lower priorities blend first; higher ones override them. With equal priorities the later one wins. |
| Blend Distance | 0 to 50 | Distance outside the box over which the influence fades to zero, in world units. |
| Weight | 0 to 1 | A multiplier on the volume's influence. Animate it to crossfade two volumes. |
| Profile Asset | `.mvolume` | A shared profile. When set, it wins over the inline profile. |

### How volumes blend

Every frame, for each camera, `VolumeManager::Evaluate` runs this sequence (<Source symbol="VolumeManager" />):

<Flow steps={["Seed from the scene's baseline", "Weigh each enabled volume at the camera", "Sort by priority, low to high", "Blend each volume's overridden slots", "Write the result to ScenePostProcess"]} caption="Volume evaluation. With no volumes the result is the baseline, unchanged." />

- Numeric values (floats and colors) **lerp** toward the volume's value by its weight.
- Discrete values (switches, integers, the AA mode) **snap** to the volume's value when its weight is at least 0.5.
- The LUT path is discrete and the LUT strength lerps, so crossing between two LUT volumes crossfades the grade.

The renderer reads the blended `ScenePostProcess` at `BeginScene`, so it needs no knowledge of volumes.

### What a profile can override

| Group | Slots |
|---|---|
| Tonemap and exposure | Tonemap on/off, exposure; auto-exposure metering, compensation, minimum and maximum EV, adaptation speeds, low and high percentiles |
| Bloom | On/off, intensity, threshold, clamp |
| SSAO | On/off, radius, intensity, bias |
| SSR | On/off, intensity, max distance, max steps, thickness, roughness cutoff |
| Anti-aliasing | Scene AA mode |
| Color grading | Contrast, saturation, temperature, lift, gamma, gain, LUT path and strength, post-process material path |
| Vignette, chromatic aberration, film grain | On/off and their parameters |
| Depth of field | On/off, focus distance, focus range, max blur |
| Motion blur | On/off, intensity, max velocity |
| Lens flare | On/off, intensity, threshold, ghost count, ghost spacing, halo radius |

## Set up a post-process volume

<Steps>
<Step title="Place a volume">
Drag a **Post Process Volume** from the **Volumes** category of the Place Actors palette into the scene.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose global or local">
Leave **Global** on for a scene-wide look. Turn it off, then set **Half Extents** to cover a room or area and **Blend Distance** for a soft transition as the camera approaches.
</Step>
<Step title="Override the settings you want">
In the component's profile, switch on the override for each setting you want to change, for example **Bloom intensity** or **Saturation**, and set its value. Everything you leave off falls through to the scene or lower-priority volumes.
</Step>
<Step title="Set the priority">
Give the local volume a higher **Priority** than your global volume so it wins inside its box.
</Step>
<Step title="Share the profile (optional)">
Save the profile as a `.mvolume` asset and assign it to **Profile Asset** on several volumes. Opening a `.mvolume` in the Volume Profile editor previews it in the viewport at full weight, above every placed volume.
</Step>
</Steps>

### Blend profiles in code

`VolumeManager::Blend` applies the same rules the renderer uses, so you can compute what a weight resolves to:

```cpp title="BlendProfiles.cpp" wrap
#include "Volumes/VolumeManager.h"
#include "Volumes/VolumeProfile.h"

Mowo::VolumeProfile result;

Mowo::VolumeProfile cave;
cave.bloomIntensity = Mowo::VolumeOverride<float>(true, 0.4f);
cave.saturation = Mowo::VolumeOverride<float>(true, 0.6f);
cave.vignetteEnabled = Mowo::VolumeOverride<bool>(true, true);

Mowo::VolumeManager::Blend(result, cave, 0.75f);

const float bloom = result.bloomIntensity.value;
const bool vignette = result.vignetteEnabled.value;

(void)bloom;
(void)vignette;
```

On disk, a profile stores only its overridden slots, so an empty profile is an empty node (<Source symbol="FVolumeProfileSerialize" />).
