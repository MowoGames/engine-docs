Global Illumination is Mowo's real-time indirect lighting. It combines a camera-centred signed distance field, a grid of irradiance probes, a radiance cache, a mesh-card surface cache and a screen-space probe gather, with temporal denoising on top. At Epic it can also trace its gather rays through the hardware ray-tracing scene. Nothing has to be baked for it to work; a baked field is only a fallback for tiers that turn the live system off.

Global Illumination is **off for a new project**. Turn it on in **Project Settings > Rendering > Features > Global Illumination** (`r.GlobalIllumination.EnableForProject 1`). Until you do, no tier, device profile or `r.*` cvar runs it, and the renderer builds none of its pipelines, volumes or probes. Once it is on it runs from the **Medium** tier up; Low turns it off and samples a [baked field](#bake-a-fallback-for-low) instead when the scene has one.

<Callout type="note">
The switch covers everything that traces the Global Illumination distance field: DDGI and screen probes, SSGI, SDF reflections, the translucent SDF reflections and the far sun shadows past the cascades. The hardware ray-traced gather also needs [Ray Tracing](/docs/rendering/ray-tracing/) on.
</Callout>

## How it fits into the frame

<Flow steps={["SDF build", "Radiance cascades", "Probe update", "…shadows, G-Buffer, lighting…", "Final gather", "Denoise", "Composite onto HDR"]} highlight={[4, 5, 6]} caption="The distance field, cascades and probes are built before the shadow pass; the gather and composite run after deferred lighting." />

The world-space builds run first, and on D3D12, Vulkan and Metal they can run on the async-compute queue while graphics draws shadows and the G-Buffer (`r.GI.AsyncCompute`, default 1). Lighting waits for them. After lighting, the gather computes indirect diffuse per pixel, denoises it and adds it to the lit image.

## The parts

### Distance field

Every ray marches a camera-centred distance field: a 3D volume built on the GPU from the scene's triangles and signed, so that thin walls do not leak light. The sign comes from a flood through the empty space from the edge of the volume, so air the flood cannot reach would read as solid; the cell around the camera is a second starting point, which keeps a room whose doors and windows are all closed lit instead of black, because the camera is always in air. Meshes can carry a field baked at cook time, which the runtime composites instead of voxelizing them. A baked field is signed from the connectivity of the open space around the mesh, not by ray parity, so an open mesh such as a single-sided wall or gallery does not turn its air into solid; fields baked by an older build heal on load. Outside its own box a mesh's field is a bound built from the value at the nearest point of the box, so the box of a large, mostly empty mesh never reads as a surface. The nearest small meshes additionally march their own baked field at its baked resolution (`r.GI.BakedSdf.DetailTrace`), which resolves wires and grilles the clipmap fattens. The field of a mesh larger than 64 clipmap cells (16 m at the 0.25 m near cell) is never put in that detail atlas, because its fixed 64-cell slot would be coarser than the clipmap and only cost fetches; the atlas itself is one persistent volume that is rewritten in place when the nearest set changes. The field is a clipmap: moving the camera only rebuilds the slab that scrolled into view. A coarser far cascade covers more distance (`r.GiCascades`, default 2; `r.GI.FarCascadeScale` sets its reach).

What enters the field:

- Static meshes, with masked (cutout) materials counted by their coverage (`r.GI.AlphaTested`).
- Skinned meshes, so a character occludes and bounces light (`r.GI.SkinnedSdf`, default 1).
- Moving physics bodies, where they stand this frame. A moving body is re-voxelized in its own box and never rebuilds the whole field or resets the radiance cache.
- Terrain and tall foliage (`r.GI.TerrainFoliage`, default 1). Foliage shorter than `r.GI.FoliageMinHeight` stays out.
- Emissive meshes are also injected as small lights, so thin emitters still light the room (`r.GI.EmissiveLights`).

### Irradiance probes

A grid of probes traces the field and stores irradiance and depth. Probes that start inside geometry are moved out. The grid is 8, 12 or 16 probes per axis depending on the tier, fitted to the scene bounds. Once lights, field and grid have been unchanged for a while, each probe updates only one frame in a few (`r.GI.DDGI.ConvergedInterval`, 4 by default): every frame traces a different quarter of the probes, so the frame cost stays even instead of the whole grid tracing on one frame in four.

### Radiance cache

A ray that hits something needs to know how bright that point is. The radiance cache stores the lit radiance of the distance field, including probe indirect and emissive light. It is rebuilt on a tier interval (`r.GI.RadianceInterval`) and only while lighting changes (`r.GI.RadianceDirty`, default 1). When the field scrolls with the camera, only the slab that scrolled in is rebaked. When static geometry changes, or a baked field recentres on the camera (every 2 m of travel in a scene larger than the field), the whole cache is rebaked at once.

Sun and light shadows inside the caches are traced through the distance field. A shadow ray that runs out of steps before it leaves the field counts as shadowed, and the caches add no constant ambient floor: sky reaches them only through probe rays that actually escape.

### Surface cache

Without a surface cache a ray hit shades from one flat colour per mesh. The surface cache captures each static mesh instance from a few oriented cards into an atlas, so a hit reads real texture detail, normals and emissive (`r.GI.SurfaceCache`, default 1). The atlas is allocated on the first captured card, so a scene with nothing to capture pays nothing. Card resolution and atlas size come from the tier.

### Final gather

How each pixel collects indirect light is set by `r.GI.FinalGather`:

- **World probes** (Medium): each pixel samples the probe grid directly.
- **Screen probes** (High and up): probes are placed on the depth buffer, one per 16-pixel tile plus extra 8-pixel probes at depth and normal edges (`r.GI.ScreenProbes.Budget`). Each probe traces first through the depth buffer (`r.GI.ScreenTraces`), then through the distance field, and shades hits from the radiance cache. Rays favour directions that were bright last frame (`r.GI.ImportanceSample`), and probes accumulate up to `r.GI.ScreenProbes.TemporalMaxFrames` (32) frames. A probe whose traced light changes by more than `r.GI.ScreenProbes.TemporalReactFloor` (0.08, relative) shortens its history so it follows the change. A tile probe whose history holds `r.GI.ScreenProbes.HalfRateMinFrames` (8) frames traces on alternate frames in a checkerboard and carries its history forward in between, each traced frame counting twice (`r.GI.ScreenProbes.HalfRateConverged`, default 1); a probe that loses its history traces every frame until it converges again.

### Hardware ray-traced gather

At Epic and Cinematic, screen-probe rays trace the ray-tracing scene with inline ray query instead of marching the distance field, which removes the field's blockiness from nearby indirect light (`r.GI.HWRTGather`). Only the trace changes: hits shade from the radiance cache. A hit the cache does not cover takes its emissive and sky irradiance but no sun, because there is no shadow ray to tell whether the sun reaches it. Without ray query it falls back to the field trace and logs why once. See [Ray tracing](/docs/rendering/ray-tracing/).

### Denoising

The gathered light goes through a velocity-aware temporal pass and a variance-guided spatial filter (`r.GI.Denoise`: `0` off, `1` on). The temporal pass keeps up to `r.GI.TemporalMaxFrames` (20) frames. It sits on top of the screen-probe history, so a lighting change drains through both. By default the filter runs at half resolution and upsamples in the composite (`r.GI.DenoiseHalfRes`), which filters with a 3×3 à-trous over the 5×5 footprint (`r.GI.DenoiseCompositeTaps 3`; `5` is the full kernel).

### Composite

The composite multiplies the gathered irradiance by the surface's diffuse albedo: the base colour times one minus metallic. This holds for world probes and screen probes alike. A fully metallic surface has no diffuse albedo, so it takes no diffuse indirect light or sky fill; its indirect light comes from reflections.

AO from GTAO or ray-traced AO darkens the indirect light here. `r.GI.AOWeight` (default 1) scales a plain AO multiply. `r.GI.BentNormalAO` (default 1) adds cone occlusion along the AO pass's bent normal, the average unoccluded direction, so creases and contacts lose indirect light while an open floor keeps its sky fill.

<Callout type="note">
In earlier versions metal surfaces such as a mirror floor picked up grey diffuse sky fill, and GTAO's bent normal on open floors pointed into the surface, which occluded most of their indirect light. Both are fixed: scenes tuned against the old look may show brighter non-metal floors and darker metals.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
Covered areas are darker than in earlier versions. The caches used to add a constant ambient floor, count a shadow ray that ran out of steps as sunlit, and, with the hardware ray-traced gather, light hits outside the cache with an unshadowed sun. All three leaked light under roofs and into galleries. Lighting changes also settle about twice as fast. If an interior tuned against the old look is now too dark, raise its lights or `r.GI.SkyScale` rather than restoring the leak.
</Callout>

### Reflections and translucency

The same cache feeds reflections. A screen-space reflection ray that leaves the screen continues through the distance field and shades from the radiance cache; at Epic a finer near cascade sharpens this (`r.GI.NearCascade`). Glass and water sample the probe field per pixel as their ambient term (`r.GI.Translucency`, default 1). See [Sky and reflections](/docs/rendering/sky-and-reflections/).

## Distance, lights and voxel worlds

**Beyond the probe window.** The live world-probe gather (Medium and the Steam Deck row) only knows the probes around the camera. Past the outermost probes the field now fades out over one probe cell and the sky's mean radiance, leaned toward the upward hemisphere, fills the rest, so a floor 100 m away no longer wears the lighting of the window's border. `r.GI.WorldProbeFarField 0` brings back the old clamp. The fill does not look at the distance field, so a far surface under cover reads as open sky; the ambient occlusion pass still darkens it. The screen-probe gather (High and up) has a far cascade instead and is not affected.

**Bounce energy.** A closed room lit by one lamp is the energy check: with the walls at albedo 0.5 the gather returns about half of the lamp-lit wall radiance a second time on Medium and High, about 0.4 of it on Epic and Cinematic on both Direct3D 12 and Vulkan, and the bounce grows with the wall colour. Every gather clamps a single ray or pixel at 64 times the scene unit; the clamps were 8 on the hardware-traced gather and the final screen-probe write, which capped a bright lamp room at a fraction of its bounce.

**Probe levels.** The world-probe field has up to three levels. Level 0 is the near window around the camera. Levels 1 and 2 are the same field at 4 times and 16 times the spacing (`r.GI.FarCascadeScale`; about 64 m and 256 m at Medium), each following the camera in whole steps of its own spacing. They exist only while the scene is wider than the near window, and `r.GI.WorldProbeLevels` sets how many run: 1 is the single near window with the fade to the sky, and it is the switch to reach for when you suspect the coarse levels. A coarse level traces at most `r.GI.DDGI.ProbeBudget` probes per frame once it is built. A scheduler picks them: a new level and the planes a scroll exposes go first and may exceed the budget, then the probes near the camera, then the probes that have waited longest, so every probe is revisited within a few frames. Everything that samples the probe field reads all the levels, blending each by how far inside its window the point sits: the world-probe gather, the specular environment pass, forward-lit translucents, the fog, the card relight, the radiance cache bake, the probe bounce and the reflection and planar captures. The screen-probe gather's own per-pixel world fallback reads the near window only. Coarse levels are not relocated away from geometry, so a probe inside a wall just carries little weight.


**Probe budget while the field changes.** A converged field already traces one probe in four. While it is changing (a scroll, a moved light, an edited mesh) Medium and the Deck row now trace one probe in two instead of all of them, each probe still updating every other frame. Set `r.GI.DDGI.MotionStride` to 1 to trace everything or up to 4 to save more; a fresh field and a probe scroll always trace everything.

**Screen-probe tile.** The screen-probe gather places one probe per 32 px tile on Low, Medium and High and per 16 px tile on Epic and Cinematic; the 8 px adaptive probes put density back at edges. `r.GI.ScreenProbes.Tile` forces 16 or 32 and is read when the renderer builds its screen-probe pipelines, so set it before the scene renders.

**Screen-probe rays at large resolutions.** The tier's rays per screen probe are sized for 1080p. Above 1080p of allocated render extent they fall in proportion, so a 4K frame traces about as many rays per frame as a 1080p one, with a floor of 32 rays per probe. `r.GI.ScreenProbes.RayBudgetPixels` sets the pixel count the rays are sized for (2073600 by default), and 0 turns the scaling off.

**Which lights feed the caches.** The caches are shaded by at most 64 lights. When a scene has more, they are ranked by power weighed against the distance from the camera to the edge of the light's reach, so one strong far light no longer evicts the lamp in the room you stand in. `r.GI.LightSelection 0` restores the global power order and `r.GI.LightSelection.Radius` sets the distance past which a light is last in line. Each light has an **Indirect Lighting Intensity** (Details > Advanced): it scales that light's contribution to the bounce, and 0 keeps the light out of the caches.

**Volume overrides.** A volume profile has a **Global Illumination** group with **Indirect Intensity** and **Bounce** multipliers that blend by priority and distance like the other overrides; the renderer still caps the bounce below 1.

**Voxel worlds.** [Voxel chunk meshes](/docs/rendering/voxel-chunks/) enter the distance field as a box per chunk, taken from the chunk bounds with the top pulled down to half of the height (`r.GI.VoxelChunks.TopFraction`), so terrain and builds occlude and bounce light. The box is a coarse stand-in: it follows the chunk bounds, not the blocks, so it is right on average and wrong in the dips of a hilly chunk. `r.GI.VoxelChunks 0` leaves chunks out as before.

**Glossy surfaces in covered rooms.** The probe field also remembers, per direction, how many of its rays escaped to the sky. A pass before the lighting (`r.GI.SpecularOcclusion`) reads it along each pixel's reflection and scales only the SKY part of the environment specular by it, so a glossy floor inside a room with no reflection capture stops mirroring the open sky. On rough surfaces (roughness 0.45 and up) `r.GI.RoughSpecular` swaps the sky prefilter for the probe field's own radiance along the reflection, so interiors reflect their bounce light. The reflection resolve subtracts exactly the term lighting added, and the diffuse ambient is untouched. A volume's **Sky Leak Floor** keeps a minimum of sky in those reflections (0 allows full occlusion) and **Update Speed** makes the probe field adapt faster or slower to a change. The View menu has **GI Sky Visibility** and **GI Rough Specular** to inspect the result. The pass needs the live probe field, so it does not run on Low's baked field.

**Screen trace.** The screen-probe gather's screen leg, the reflection trace and the hardware ray-traced gather take twice the fine steps and leaps over stretches where the ray stays in front of the nearest surface of every 16 px tile it crosses (`r.GI.ScreenTrace.Hzb 0` restores the linear march). Thin occluders are missed less often at about the same cost.

**Baked field format.** A `.mgi` bake is version 2: it carries the coarse probe levels after the near one and records whether its depth atlas holds sky visibility. Version 1 bakes still load. A scene wider than the near window can be baked once the coarse levels are built, and the world-probe gather samples the baked coarse levels the same way as live ones.

**Surface-cache size.** The mesh-instance cap of the surface cache (`r.GI.SurfaceCache.Instances`) is now a tier value. Every tier keeps the 64 it always had; set the cvar above 0 to raise it for a scene with many hero meshes.

## Settings

- **Project Settings > Rendering > Features > Global Illumination**: the project switch. Off by default; the rest of this list applies once it is on.
- **Project Settings > Rendering**: **Dynamic GI Method** (off, screen space or radiance cascades; needs a restart) and **Reflection Method**.
- **Project Settings > Engine > Global Illumination**: the distance field resolution, radiance interval, denoise mode, final gather, probe and ray counts, surface cache, the tracing step caps, the screen-probe settings, and an **Ambient Occlusion** section (AO slices, the ray-traced AO rays, radius scale, calibration and history, **AO Weight On Indirect**, **Bent Normal Occlusion** and the AO and bent-normal resolutions).
- Players get a **Global Illumination** row (off, screen space or radiance cascades) in the **Graphics** settings. In a game that leaves the project switch off it is greyed out with "this game does not use it".

## Tune it for a scene

<Steps>
<Step title="Check that it is running">
Make sure **Global Illumination** is on in Project Settings and the tier is Medium or higher, or force it with `r.DDGI 1`. Switch the viewport to the **Global Illumination** buffer view (or set `r.ViewMode 14`) to see the indirect light on its own.
</Step>
<Step title="Balance sky and AO">
`r.GI.SkyScale` (default 1) scales how much sky light fills outdoor shadows. `r.GI.AOWeight` (default 1) controls how strongly AO darkens the indirect light. Neither changes metals: a fully metallic surface takes no diffuse fill, so light it through its reflections instead.
</Step>
<Step title="Adjust the indirect intensity">
`SceneRenderer::Options::DdgiIntensity` is an artist gain on indirect diffuse.
</Step>
<Step title="Inspect the distance field">
If light leaks or thin objects bounce no light, look at the field itself with the SDF debug view, which replaces the image with a sphere-traced view of the field. Raise `r.GI.SDFResolution` for a larger, finer field.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Bake a fallback for Low

Low turns the live system off. Without a bake such a machine lights every room with flat sky light. A baked field stores the converged probe grid beside the scene as `<scene>.mgi`, and Low samples it at the cost of one fullscreen pass (`r.GI.Baked`, default 1).

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the scene">
Frame the area you want baked: the bake is taken from the live probe field, which covers the scene while it fits the probe window.
</Step>
<Step title="Run Build Lighting">
Run **Build Lighting** from the command palette. The editor turns the live system on, waits in the viewport until it converges, and writes the field beside the scene. A notification shows progress and has a **Cancel** button.
</Step>
<Step title="Ship it">
The `.mgi` file sits next to the scene file and ships with it. Rebuild after you change lighting or geometry.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Limits

- **The field is camera-centred and finite.** Geometry outside its window is not in the field that rays march; the far cascade extends the reach at lower detail.
- **Screen-probe tracing starts in screen space.** Off-screen geometry is reached only through the field or, at Epic, the ray-tracing scene.
- **Probe count changes take effect on the next launch** (`r.DDGI.ProbesPerAxis`). Ray budgets and the radiance interval change live.
- **Low has only the baked field**, which does not react to moving lights.
